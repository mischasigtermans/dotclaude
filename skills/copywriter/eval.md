# Eval

Run this once the rewrite is done. Mark every check pass or fail, and fix the draft before you return it if anything fails.

On a detect request, the response names every pattern it found, with the quoted line and a short fix, and leaves the draft unedited.

## Editing principles

1. Is the user's point intact, with no claim, example, statistic, quote or opinion added?
2. Does the writer's own voice survive: the words they pick, their cadence, how blunt, funny or unsure they are, where they digress and how polished they are?
3. Were strong human sentences left as they were, with no rewrite for consistency and no paragraph tidied to match the rest?
4. Does the amount cut match the amount of slop, with no heavy compression that removes character?
5. Does the draft open with what the reader needs, and keep the personal setup that brings context, tension or character?
6. Is the point up front wherever that makes it clearer, without every unit forced into one shape?
7. Does each sentence earn its place? Where the draft allows, are facts concrete, details protected and verbs direct?
8. Is it in the active voice, with human subjects wherever they fit?
9. Does the edge survive, and does the structure stay unless it was hurting the piece?
10. Did you untangle the sentences that were hard to follow, and keep clear spoken cadence, fragments and shifts in pace?

## Words to cut

1. Are the words banned by the user's own instructions, filler phrases, adverbs that add nothing and inflated claims gone, unless the draft quotes them as an example?

## Patterns to cut

On a product page, the two headline exceptions at the top of `patterns.md` hold: a headline fragment or a pair of true opposites passes these checks.

1. Did you remove every throat-clearing opener, rhetorical setup, binary contrast and negative listing?
2. Have you fixed every faux-insight setup, colon reveal, fake-strong verb and case of superficial analysis, synonym cycling, dramatic fragmentation or robotic rhythm?
3. Has importance puffery become plain fact, and weasel attribution a named source? Where nobody can name a source, did you flag the claim to the user?
4. Are fake-profound kickers deleted, not reworked into a better metaphor?
5. Is any summary-recap ending gone, leaving the piece to stop on its last concrete point, takeaway or next action?
6. Is the formatting slop gone: emoji in headings, decorative bold, bullets that belong in prose, headers on tiny sections?
7. Does the text after each colon start in sentence case, unless a title, a proper noun, code or grammar calls for a capital?
8. Do dashes follow the user's own rule? Without one, are em dashes rare, with none in short copy as a rule and 1-2 at most in a longer draft, each one clearly helping?

## Final read

1. Did you check the edit against this file yourself, with no separate editor and evaluator agents?
2. Is the draft free of robotic symmetry, sentences cut to one shape and punchy fragments stacked in a row?
3. Would the writer recognize the edit as their own voice?
4. Would it sound natural read aloud to a sharp colleague?
5. Does the output match the way the draft came in? A pasted draft comes back whole and edited; a file edited on disk gets a before and after table that names it. In both cases, is there a short 'What changed' section?
6. On a detect request, does every finding give the pattern, its quoted line and a short fix, with no rewrite, no score and no claim about AI authorship?
