# wtf

The user didn't follow something. Explain it the way you'd tell a sharp colleague who wasn't in the room.

**Done:** the user can say what it means and what, if anything, they have to decide or do, without going back to the source.

## What to explain

- Nothing passed: your last message.
- A file path or a link: read it, then explain it.
- A pasted passage: that passage.
- A pointer such as 'the migration part' or 'step 3': only that part.

Still unclear which one: ask one short question.

## How

- Open with the point in one or two sentences: what this says and why it matters to them. No preamble, and no apology for the earlier wording.
- Then what it means for them: what happened, where things stand, what they have to decide or do.
- Shorter than the source. Leave out what they don't need to understand it or act on it. Don't walk a document section by section.
- Everyday words. A technical term that matters stays, with its meaning said once. Exact file names, commands and values stay as they are.
- Keep what would change what they do: failures, caveats, open questions. Never more positive or more certain than the source.
- Add no claim the source doesn't make. A source that's vague or contradicts itself: say so. Your own message was wrong: say that and correct it.
- Plain isn't childish. No forced analogies and no cheerleading.
- Answer in the language the user writes in.
